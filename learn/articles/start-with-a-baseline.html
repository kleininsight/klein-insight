Before you try to grow, establish a baseline.

If you cannot describe how your business performs today, it's difficult to tell whether a new initiative actually improved it.

1. Define the decision

Begin with a specific question, such as whether to hire another employee, change pricing, or invest in advertising. Different decisions need different measurements.

2. Record what happens today

Choose a small group of metrics you can measure consistently. Examples include qualified leads, lead-to-customer conversion, gross margin, time spent handling escalations, and repeat purchases. Use only metrics relevant to your decision.

3. Separate facts from assumptions

For each metric, note its source, time period, and reliability. If you do not have reliable data, identify what must be collected rather than guessing.

4. Establish the comparison

Record your baseline, the change you intend to make, the result you expect, and when you will evaluate it. Account for seasonality and other changes where possible.

5. Review and adjust

Compare results with the baseline, but don't assume that every change was caused by your intervention. Investigate alternative explanations before committing more resources.

Try this exercise

  1. Write down one decision you need to make in the next 30 days.
  2. Identify three indicators that could inform it.
  3. For each, write where the data comes from and what period it covers.
  4. Write one action you will take if the evidence supports your hypothesis, and another if it does not.

What this framework does not do: It cannot guarantee business growth or establish causality on its own. It helps you make decisions more transparently.
