The Owner-Bottleneck Stage

A good problem to have can still be a problem.

Your involvement helped build the business. Customers trusted you, employees came to you, and you made the important decisions. Eventually, growth creates a different challenge: too much of the business still depends on one person.

  • Every important decision still comes through you
  • Growth creates more work for you instead of more capacity
  • Your team can execute, but still relies on you for direction
  • Daily operations leave little time to improve how the business runs
  • The business works because you make it work

This is not evidence that you built the business incorrectly. It is evidence that the business has reached the point where the way it operates needs to evolve.

How Coaching Works

Bring the real business problem. Leave with a clearer next move.

Each session starts with what is happening in the business now. There is no lesson to get through. We work the highest-value issue, make a decision or build a plan, and use the next meeting to review what happened and adjust.

01

Start With What Changed

Review what moved since the last session: results, roadblocks, numbers, decisions, team issues, or new opportunities. That determines where the conversation should go.

02

Work the Highest-Value Issue

Pressure-test assumptions, look at the evidence, compare options, and get specific about the tradeoffs. When more information is needed, research or analysis becomes part of the work.

03

Turn the Decision Into Action

Leave with clear priorities, ownership, and next steps. At the next session, we review what happened, learn from it, and either keep pushing or move to the next constraint.

The Topic Can Change. The Standard Does Not.

Some sessions may focus on one major decision. Others may move across operations, marketing, people, pricing, or growth. The goal is always the same: make the next decision better and move the business forward.
