About Me

The common thread throughout my life has been helping people move forward.

B.A., Managerial Economics
University of California, Davis

Former Senior Market Research Specialist
11 years at SMUD

Running a business is one of the loneliest, hardest challenges you can take on—and I know that firsthand.

Before launching this practice, I spent a decade as a professional market researcher and strategist. On any given day, I was managing a crushing workload of 10 to 12 major marketing and research initiatives. These weren't repetitive tasks; they were highly complex, vastly different projects that required intense cognitive energy. I was performing at an elite level, but the corporate machine was consuming me. After five years of sustaining that pace, I looked in the mirror and realized I was literally going grey in my beard from the stress.

The breaking point—my epiphany—came when my wife got pregnant.

I was commuting an hour each way, completely drained. By the time I walked through the front door, I was physically present but mentally absent. I only had enough energy to eat dinner and maybe watch an episode of The Big Bang Theory with my wife before falling asleep on the couch.

Then, the reality of the situation hit me like a ton of bricks: If I am too burnt out to be the supportive partner my pregnant wife deserves right now, how the hell am I going to be a present, energized father to a newborn?

I refused to let my career rob my family of the man they needed. I made the radical decision to leave that stable, high-paying corporate role. I didn't leave because I lost my passion for strategy—I left because I wanted to apply that decade of elite marketing, data, and market research experience to something that actually matters: helping driven, ambitious independent business owners unlock their true potential.

If you are currently sacrificing your health, your family, or your sanity to keep your business growing, I know exactly what that feels like. Let's fix the bottlenecks, build an scalable engine, and get your life back.

My job is to help you be successful in whatever you are trying to accomplish.

The Owner-Bottleneck Stage

A good problem to have can still be a problem.

Your involvement helped build the business. Customers trusted you, employees came to you, and you made the important decisions. Eventually, growth creates a different challenge: too much of the business still depends on one person.

We will audit and strip away the friction across your entire ecosystem to find your operational ceiling.

How dependent is the business on you today?

  • Can you take a two-week vacation without worrying that things would stop moving while you were gone?
  • How often do your employees make important decisions without waiting for your final approval?
  • Do you find yourself “just doing it yourself” because you know you can do it faster or better?
  • When was the last time you delegated something and did not hear about it again until it was done?
  • Are you spending more of your time keeping the business running than actually growing it?
  • If the business grew significantly tomorrow, would that create more freedom for you or just more work?

I believe most business owners are struggling with one or more of these issues and have simply found ways to “make it work.”

It does not have to stay that way.

As the business grows, the goal is not to keep absorbing more responsibility yourself. It is to build the systems, people, decision-making, and structure that allow the business to keep moving without everything depending on you.
