Do you really know how to use AI? Because knowing how to use a hammer doesn't make you a carpenter. I started using ChatGPT a couple of months after its release. At the time, I didn't really understand what it was capable of. I mostly used it as a joke, like having it write a country song about my friend missing a golf putt in real time. But I quickly learned that AI is an incredibly powerful tool. I also started noticing how many people put their blind trust in it. I've spent over a decade working in market research and data analysis, and AI has dramatically changed what I can accomplish and how quickly I can do it. But here's the difference. You probably can't use AI for market research the way I can. Just like I can't use AI for computer programming the way an experienced programmer can. When I use AI for #marketresearch , I know how to ask the right questions, structure my prompts, and guide it toward what I actually need. But even with all that experience, I rarely accept the first answer. I challenge its assumptions, question its methodology, identify weaknesses, and go through multiple rounds of revisions and refinement before I use anything. And here's the important part: I know what to look for because I understand the subject. Someone without that background might look at the exact same output and think it's brilliant. And that's where I think people need to be careful. AI can produce an incredibly convincing answer that's completely wrong. And if you don't understand the subject, how would you know? Now, don't get me wrong. One of the greatest things about AI is its ability to help people learn things they previously knew nothing about. I'm constantly using it to expand my own knowledge. But there's a difference between using #AI to develop your expertise and using AI as a substitute for expertise. The carpenter and I have access to the same hammer. The difference isn't the tool. It's the knowledge, experience, and judgment behind it. AI doesn't eliminate the value of expertise. It amplifies it.
